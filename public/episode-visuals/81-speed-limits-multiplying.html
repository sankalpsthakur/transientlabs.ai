Where did the fourth product go?

Write X=10h+l and Y=10j+k. Schoolbook expansion uses hj, hk, lj and lk. Karatsuba uses H=hj, L=lk and S=(h+l)(j+k). Then S−H−L=hk+lj, so XY=100H+10(S−H−L)+L. For 12×34: H=3, L=8, S=21, middle=10, answer=408.

The four-place window includes leading zeros and normal decimal carries. First carry floor(L/10) into the middle coefficient; then carry floor((middle+first carry)/10) into H. Even when S reaches 324 or middle reaches 162, the identity is exact. All results are shown at every stage: the manual sequence is explanation, not measured computation time.

This independently authored two-place integer model is not a recursive multiplier, a 25% timing or bit-operation saving, an FFT implementation, a new asymptotic speed limit, or a paper/Lean fidelity audit. Adding chunks can add a digit; additions, subtraction and carry handling cost work. The recursive algorithm's improvement needs separate complexity analysis. Manual sequencing stops after recombination without wrapping.
