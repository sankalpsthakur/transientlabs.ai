What this register explains

Start at−m. Adding n positive signs gives−m+n; negative signs give−m−n. At n=m, the totals are0 or−2m. The dial stays−16 to16.

Planar illustration: a=(1,0), b=σ(cosθ,sinθ), det[a,b]=σsinθ. Reversing b flips the sign for0<θ<180°. At0/180°, collinear branches have no crossing sign. With n0, total−m remains valid; otherwise the undefined sign suppresses the total.

The contact banks count integers. Manual additions stop atn=m; reducing m clamps n and its control limit together. Three lamps show a synthetic route. Zero count is one prerequisite, not a proof certificate or truth verdict.

This is not a reconstruction of higher-dimensional cusp geometry or a check of Eliashberg–Murphy, Lean or a withdrawn theorem. A failed argument and a false statement are separate claims.
