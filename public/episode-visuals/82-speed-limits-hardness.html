What counts as a sensitive block?

Four rows have four bits each. A row fires only at 1100 or 0011; the output ORs all rows. Decimal codes 0–15 encode left-to-right weights 8,4,2,1. Bits 1–16 follow rows A–D.

s(x) counts single flips that change output. bs(x) is the largest disjoint collection of nonempty sensitive groups, each flipped independently—not all sensitive groups. At all zeros: s(x)=0, bs(x)=8. This function's global maxima are s(f)=4, bs(f)=8. Fixed pairs are candidates; the maximum witness contains only sensitive groups.

A nonfiring row has two complementary flip sets reaching 1100 and 0011; four rows give eight disjoint blocks. With output 1, every firing row must be disabled, allowing four disjoint blocks. Each test starts from the unchanged base; scans stop at the last candidate without wrapping.
